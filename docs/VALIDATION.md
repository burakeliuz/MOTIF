# Validation: what was checked, how, and what it shows

Phase 10 of the engine refactor (2026-10-07). Numbers come from
`reports/final_validation.md` (generated by `python3 tools/validation_report.py
--write reports/final_validation.md`), `reports/continuous_engine_analysis.md`
(phase 6), and `reports/continuous_holdout.md` (phase 7, pre-registered). All
checks here are offline: stored Qloo responses, no Qloo request, no LLM call.
The phase-10 numbers are for `lexicon-0.3` and `sensory-1.0`; §2.6 gives the
effect of `lexicon-0.4` and `sensory-1.1`, which were not validated anew.

**What validation can and cannot say.** There is no ground truth for "the right
scent of a brand". Nothing here claims that a direction is correct, preferred,
or will be liked. The checks test what can be tested: that the engine is
deterministic and traceable, that different evidence gives different results
(and the same evidence the same result), that small changes in evidence give
small changes in output, that it holds on brands it was not built on, and how it
compares to the frozen rule engine and to an LLM working alone.

## 1. Methods

| Check | Method | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Unit, web, review, browser, PDF | `python3 -m unittest` (network blocked); the browser test drives headless Chromium on loopback, prints the brief to A4 and counts pages | `tests/` |
| Determinism | the 13 trial brands run twice in one process: identical canonical JSON | report §1 |
| Repeatability | the same 13 runs in two fresh interpreters with different hash seeds: same SHA-256 | report §1 |
| Collapse, stage by stage | distinct per-brand sets at each stage, legacy (frozen baseline) vs continuous (the web app's path: the continuous controller on the recordings) | report §2 |
| Separation | Euclidean distance between commitment vectors (value × confidence per resolved dimension); overlap (Jaccard) of chosen directions between brands that both propose something | report §3 |
| Forcing check | (a) the same with the low-confidence design cells removed; (b) Spearman correlation between the overlap of two brands' literal Qloo descriptors and the overlap of their outputs | report §3 |
| Perturbation | every annotated Qloo descriptor left out once (523 runs) and every related brand or film left out once (260 runs): do resolved labels or the architecture change? | report §4 |
| Holdout | five brands never used in building, pre-registered before any request (phase 7); replayed here with the scent architecture added post hoc (the olfactory layer was built after that run and never saw these brands) | report §5, `reports/continuous_holdout.md` |
| LLM-only baseline | existing structured answers of the stage-6B trial (arm C: Claude alone, no Qloo data) for Balenciaga, Gucci, Harley-Davidson, Sanrio; per dimension, how often each method commits and whether two methods agree where both commit | report §6 |
| Recorded-data review of the pages | 17 brands (13 trial, 4 resolved holdout) through the web flow and the printed brief: pages, overflow, internal wording, repeated text, context line | `docs/REFACTOR_LOG.md` phase 9 |

**LLM-only budget.** No new LLM call was made. The configured daily cap is 20
calls (`MOTIF_LLM_MAX_CALLS`, ledger `data/llm_calls.jsonl`) and 11 were already
used on 2026-10-07; a fresh 13-brand LLM-only run would exceed it. The existing
four structured answers are used instead. The first trial's two LLM-only answers
(Aesop, Supreme) are free text and were not turned into dimensions, because that
reading would be MOTIF's interpretation, not the model's answer.

## 2. Results

### 2.1 Determinism and the gates

- Deterministic in process and across interpreters (same SHA-256).
- Every contributor of every resolved dimension maps to Qloo evidence with a
  request ID and a JSON pointer (13 of 13 trial brands; holdout 4 of 4).
- The holdout has no structural failure under its pre-registered definition.

### 2.2 Collapse: legacy vs continuous (13 trial brands)

| Stage | Legacy engine-0.3 | Continuous |
|---|---|---|
| Qloo descriptors | 13 | 13 |
| Lexicon cue groups | 13 | 13 |
| Active / leading motifs | 12 | 12 |
| Sensory profile | 7 | 11 |
| Eligible materials / directions | 7 | 12 |
| Selected materials / scent architecture | **3** | **11** |

The legacy engine collapsed after the motif stage (12 motif sets → 7 targets
→ 3 material sets). The continuous engine keeps 11 distinct profiles and 11
distinct architectures. Remaining collisions: profiles A24 = Supreme and
Comme des Garçons = Balenciaga; architectures Comme des Garçons = Balenciaga and
Patagonia = Aesop. Nike has no architecture (no motif leads). Between two brands
that both propose something, the chosen directions overlap 0.12 on average (66
pairs); the legacy selected materials overlapped 0.55 (10 pairs).

### 2.3 Real differentiation or forcing?

Evidence that the differences are not arbitrary:

- Collisions follow identical or near-identical profiles, and no role on the 13
  brands was decided by a tie-break (no two eligible directions had an equal
  rank; the closest call is Harley-Davidson's core, 0.579 vs 0.577).
- The architecture follows the profile's values, not only its labels: of the
  523 single-descriptor runs, 14 change both, 15 only the labels (the
  structure holds), and 6 only the architecture (a value moves enough to reorder
  two directions without changing any label).
- Small perturbations change little: leaving one annotated descriptor out
  changes the resolved labels in 6% of 523 runs and the architecture in 4%;
  leaving one related brand or film out, 7% and 5% of 260 runs.

Evidence that part of it rests on MOTIF's own design readings, not on data:

- With the low-confidence (tentative) cells removed, 9 profiles and **7
  architectures** remain (Nike and Sanrio get none). Six of the 13
  architectures rest only on tentative leanings and are labelled so in the
  product.
- The overlap of two brands' literal Qloo descriptors barely predicts the
  overlap of their outputs (Spearman 0.08 over 66 pairs; with profile distance
  −0.16 over 78 pairs). Literal descriptor overlap ignores synonyms, so this is a
  weak test, but it gives no support for a strong claim that output similarity
  tracks evidence similarity.

Reading: the continuous engine reduced collapse without random spreading (stable
under perturbation, deterministic, traceable), but about a third of the
differentiation between architectures (11 → 7) comes from tentative design
cells. The product labels those roles "tentative"; they are MOTIF's creative
reading, not measured facts.

### 2.4 Holdout (five new brands)

- Pre-registered results: 4 of 5 resolved (IKEA: Qloo's untyped search returns
  only stores); 3 distinct profiles among 4; misreads 5 of 59 descriptors (8%),
  mostly "industrial design".
- Scent architecture, added post hoc: Hermès and Bang & Olufsen receive
  supported structures; LEGO and Coca-Cola share one tentative structure
  (aldehydic / powdery iris / creamy woods), the same as Ralph Lauren, because
  each rests on a single "slightly polished" texture. Weak profiles converge.

### 2.5 Three-way comparison on four brands

| | Legacy | Continuous | LLM only (Claude, no Qloo) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dimensions committed (of 24) | 3 | 10 | 19 |
| Agreement with the LLM where both commit | 2 of 3 | 7 of 8 | — |
| Distinct profiles (of 4) | 3 | 4 | 4 |
| Traceable to a source | yes | yes | no |
| Audience or historical claims | none | none | yes (stage-6B notes) |

The LLM commits to almost every dimension (warm for three of the four brands);
the continuous engine commits where its evidence and model reach and leaves the
rest open. Where both commit they mostly agree. This agreement is not
validation of either: both can share the same cultural associations.

### 2.6 After `lexicon-0.4` and `sensory-1.1` (2026-10-07)

Energy and technology motifs, two precision words, and a narrative-tone context
rule (`docs/REFACTOR_LOG.md`), replayed offline on the 19 recorded brands:
resolved dimensions change for 7, the architecture for 5; Nike, previously
without a direction, gets one; distinct architectures 12 → 15; MUJI, Aesop,
Le Labo, A24, Supreme, Sanrio, Balenciaga, and Hermès are unchanged. Not a
validation: the new words were chosen by reading these same recordings, and no
new holdout was run. Two near-threshold side effects are reported as found
(Harley-Davidson's drydown, Coca-Cola's impression).

## 3. Where Qloo adds what a plain LLM does not

- **Traceable evidence.** Every motif, dimension, and direction traces to a Qloo
  entity, field, request, and date. The LLM-only briefs cannot be traced.
- **The brand's own descriptors and its references, separately.** Qloo returns
  the brand's own entry and the brands and films it relates to; MOTIF weighs
  them separately and says when a direction comes only from the references
  (Hermès: polished texture comes from related brands such as Fendi and Tod's,
  not from Hermès's own entry).
- **Honest gaps.** With Qloo evidence the engine leaves dimensions open; the LLM
  fills them from memory.

What Qloo does not supply by itself: the translation into scent. That step is
MOTIF's model (`sensory-1.1`, `olfactory-1.1`), partly backed by open odor
data and partly design opinion, and labelled as such. The stage-6B trial also
showed that Qloo's own entries carry character MOTIF's lexicon does not read yet
(LEGO, Coca-Cola, Harley-Davidson's "Rugged" and "Chrome Details").

## 5. Not claimed

- No direction is "correct"; nothing was smelled or rated by a perfumer.
- No preference, audience, or market prediction.
- Agreement with the LLM-only baseline is not evidence of truth.
- Numbers are from 13 trial brands and 5 holdout brands; small samples.
