# Collapse analysis: where distinct Qloo profiles become the same scent output

Offline diagnostic, 2026-10-07, branch `claude/wizardly-carson-y19ryg` (product code
at `620072b`). **No Qloo request, no LLM call, no change to engine behaviour,
rules, lexicon, palette, or parameters.** The 13 brands of `reports/trial_6b.md`
are replayed from their stored live responses through the unchanged engine
(`RecordedQloo`); `tools/collapse_analysis.py` only reads the engine's own
results and the stored response bodies.

Reproduce (needs the git-ignored recordings under `data/`):

```sh
python3 tools/collapse_analysis.py --json /tmp/collapse.json --markdown /tmp/tables.md
```

Inputs per brand: MUJI, A24, Comme des Garçons, Nike, Ralph Lauren from the
stage-2 run (`live-20261006T103307Z-660d`); Le Labo (brand entity chosen) and
Patagonia from the T1 sessions; Aesop, Supreme, Gucci, Harley-Davidson, Sanrio,
Balenciaga from the 6B/trial web sessions. Balenciaga's weight conflict is
answered "leave open", as in the trial. The controller fetched own entry,
related brands, and related films for every brand (no domain was skipped).
The requested per-brand pipeline (Qloo input, classification retention,
translation retention, material retention) and the cross-brand comparison are
generated by the script and printed in Appendix G (G.1–G.9); sections 2–4 are
the build agent's reading of those numbers.

The **first identical pair** appears at **active motifs** (MUJI = Aesop). The
**largest drop** is **motif → sensory targets** (12 → 7 unique profiles; 8 of the
9 pairs that become identical before the final stage do so here). The second
largest is **targets → final materials** (7 → 3), mostly because brands with one
direction receive no composition at all.

## 2. Reading the measurements

**Qloo input (A).** Every brand gets about 200–225 evidence items from 1 own
entity, 10 related brands, and 10 related films; distinct descriptor names per
brand are 122–143 and the 13 descriptor sets share little (mean Jaccard 0.12;
MUJI–Aesop 0.15, Harley-Davidson–Sanrio 0.07). Even pairs that end identical
start clearly apart. The own entry is small in what MOTIF reads: 9–16 of
98–176 own tags per brand (162 of 1,677 in total). Related brands and films carry
about 94% of the read evidence. Affinity ranges are narrow (0.88–0.99) and are
not used.

**Extraction (B).** On the fetched entities Qloo returns 49,934 tags; the
namespaces MOTIF reads hold 2,721 (5%). Most unread types are not aesthetic
(products, markets, demographics, competitors, `keyword:media`), but several
are cultural descriptors MOTIF never sees: related brands' `personal_style`
(386 tags; read only on the own entry), `core_value` (573), `cultural_relevance`
(412), `design_inspiration` (303), `lifestyle` (1,020), `material` (360); films'
`theme` (1,086), `character` (1,302), `plot` (1,129), `keyword:qloo` (1,368);
on the own entry `lifestyle`, `keyword`, `core_value`, `cultural_relevance`
(about 30–60 tags each across the 13 brands). The descriptive **property
arrays** (`aesthetic_properties`, `emotional_tone`, `personal_style`) duplicate
existing tags exactly (1,576 of 1,576 values have a same-name tag), so reading
them adds nothing; the free-text fields (`style_description`,
`cultural_context_description`, films' `emotional_tone_description`,
`plot_themes_description`) are unread and would need a parser, not a lexicon.
Related music artists were recorded for the five stage-2 brands (498–896 tags
each) but are outside the default domains.

**Lexicon (C).** 523 of 2,721 items (19%) match any cue; per brand 8% (Nike) to
30% (MUJI, Comme des Garçons). Only 244 annotations count as support (the rest
are common cues, excluded contexts, or negations). Brands whose own entry speaks
a vocabulary outside the 47 cue groups keep almost nothing: Sanrio and Nike end
with one support cue group, Harley-Davidson with five ("Rugged", "Chrome
Details", "motorcycle leathers" match no cue).

**Activation (D).** Strength counts distinct source kinds, so very different
amounts of support become the same label: Comme des Garçons' provocation (17
items, 8 entities, 4 cue groups) and A24's (3 items, 2 entities) are both
"moderate"; Gucci's precision on 10 related entities is "moderate" like
Patagonia's naturalness on 2. Pooled: 243 support items → 202 entities → 99
counted units. This is where MUJI and Aesop meet: MUJI's restraint (12 items,
3 kinds, own included) and Aesop's (6 items, 2 kinds, related only) both just
"activate". The graded information is computed and then discarded.

**Translation (E).** 30 active motifs, 18 mapped (60%). The 12 unmapped ones are
provocation ×5, heritage ×2, experimentation ×2, playfulness, industrial
character, romance. Four brands lose every active motif here (A24, Harley-
Davidson, Sanrio, plus Nike with none) and become identical "no direction"
profiles, although their descriptor sets have Jaccard 0.06–0.22 with each other.
Mapped motifs reach exactly one pole each, so any two brands with the same
mapped subset get the same targets regardless of what else they carry: Comme
des Garçons and Balenciaga (polished; descriptor Jaccard 0.35) and Ralph Lauren
and Gucci (dense, polished; 0.50). Only 4 of the 12 poles are reached in practice
(light, dense, polished, natural; the intimate rule exists but its cues are common
in the reference set); temperature, projection, and sweetness are unknown for all
13 brands.

**Materials (F, G).** The eligible step keeps every distinction it receives (7 →
7): eligibility and scores are a direct function of the target set, and
strength changes scores without changing order (MUJI 0.667 vs Aesop 0.533 for
HEDIONE). With 7 verified materials, 2–4 are eligible per profile. Composition
then removes more: four brands with one target (Comme des Garçons, Patagonia,
Supreme, Balenciaga) get no materials by the two-target gate, and Le Labo (light,
polished) gets the same three as MUJI and Aesop (light, polished, natural)
because fill-to-three adds bergamot. The final stage holds 3 profiles, one of
them "nothing" for 8 brands.

## 3. Information-loss ranking (most severe first)

1. **E. Motif → sensory translation** — 12 → 7 unique profiles; 40% of active
   motifs dropped; 4 brands emptied; single-pole rules merge two more pairs; 8
   of 12 poles never reached.
2. **G. Composition selection** — 7 → 3; the two-target gate empties 4 brands
   that have a direction; fill rules merge Le Labo into MUJI/Aesop.
3. **D. Activation/support model** — first identical pair (MUJI = Aesop); graded
   support (1–17 items, 1–10 entities, 1–6 cue groups per motif) reduced to
   active/inactive and two strength labels.
4. **C. Lexicon/classification** — 81% of read items unmatched; 47 cue groups for
   12 motifs; vocabulary-poor brands (Nike, Sanrio, Harley-Davidson) keep 1–5
   support cues. It narrows diversity (mean Jaccard 0.12 → 0.30) but keeps all 13
   profiles distinct.
5. **F. Material palette/profile** — no loss of its own at the eligibility step
   (7 → 7), but the ceiling is low: 7 materials, 2–4 eligible per profile, no
   verified material for 3 poles (synthetic, cool, intimate). It cannot recover distinctions lost upstream.
6. **B. Descriptor extraction** — 95% of tags unread (90% on the own entry),
   including culturally descriptive types; yet what is read is already distinct
   for all 13 brands, so this limits richness, not distinctness.
7. **A. Qloo data** — least severe: evidence differs strongly between brands.
   Caveats: the own entry is small in what MOTIF reads, related entities
   dominate (about 94% of read items), ten per domain.

## 4. Answers

1. **Is Qloo producing sufficiently different cultural evidence?** Yes. All 13
   evidence sets are unique (mean Jaccard 0.12–0.14 on the read evidence and on
   all `:qloo` descriptor tags); the 32 pairs that end with identical materials
   start at descriptor Jaccard 0.04–0.50.
2. **How much Qloo information is discarded before motif extraction?** By
   count, 95% of tags on the fetched entities are never read (49,934 → 2,721; on
   the own entry 1,677 → 162), and of what is read 81% matches no cue (2,721 →
   523; 244 counted as support). So about 0.5% of returned tags end up as motif
   support. Much of the unread volume is non-aesthetic; the culturally
   descriptive unread part is still several thousand tags (section 6).
3. **Where is the largest collapse?** Motif → axis: 12 → 7 unique profiles (and
   60% motif retention). Descriptor → motif loses volume but keeps all brands
   distinct; axis → eligible material loses nothing; eligible → final material
   collapses further (7 → 3) mainly through the composition gate.
4. **Would expanding the material palette alone solve it?** No. Final distinctness
   cannot exceed the 7 target profiles, 4 brands have no target to match, and
   brands with identical targets (MUJI/Aesop, Ralph Lauren/Gucci, Comme des
   Garçons/Balenciaga) would still get identical rankings. A larger palette
   could only separate cases like MUJI vs Le Labo and fill empty poles.
5. **Would more one-off R6-style rules solve the structural problem?** No. Each
   rule maps one binary motif to one pole; it can move one brand out of the
   "no direction" group but adds no gradation, and with equal-weight conflicts it
   turns co-present motifs into conflicts (measured for R6: three new conflicts
   in 13 brands). Brands sharing the same mapped subset stay identical.
6. **Is the weighted motif vector → continuous 6D sensory vector idea supported?**
   Partly yes. The data carries graded motif signal that the model discards
   (section 6, D), and the dominant collapse is the binary, one-pole, 4-of-12-pole
   translation (E). A weighted translation that gives every motif a contribution
   over several dimensions would keep MUJI and Aesop apart (different weights)
   and give the four empty brands a direction. Two cautions from the numbers:
   the weights are confounded by lexicon coverage and by related-entity volume
   (related evidence is about 94% of what is read), and the composition gate and the
   7-material palette would still compress the output (G, F). Distinct output
   is not the same as correct output; no human rating exists.

## Recommended next intervention

**Change the motif → sensory translation layer first** (diagnosis E): replace
"one active motif → one pole" with a versioned mapping in which each of the 12
motifs contributes to the six dimensions, so unmapped motifs stop vanishing and
co-present motifs combine instead of colliding. It is the stage with the largest
measured loss (12 → 7, 40% of active motifs dropped) and the first one at which
most collisions occur; it can be evaluated offline on these 13 recordings with
the same script before anything else changes. Retaining motif weights (D) is the
natural second step, since only one collision (MUJI = Aesop) originates there.

Not implemented. The mapping would be a new design rule set (version bump,
written reason, owner approval), and these 13 brands would stop being
independent checks for it.
